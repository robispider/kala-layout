## 2. Integer model (task 1)

Frozen rule (`search/integer.py`): raw keystroke and pair counts of the training tables, K̂ = 10^6 K, M̂ = 10^6 M, B̂ = 10^6 B. The grid stores K with six decimals and B is a multiple of 0.1, so every coefficient is an exact integer (the code refuses any value that is not) and J = Z / (N · 10^6) holds exactly. A bound on the integer objective Z is therefore a bound on J with no rounding error. The only rounding left is the grid's: its efforts are the Fitts formula rounded to six decimals, which moves any layout's J1 by at most the bound below.

* **LB0** (RESEARCH_SPEC 8.3): the linear part at its rearrangement minimum and every searched pair at the smallest penalty (−0.2).
* **Gilmore–Lawler** (`search/bounds.py`): a linear assignment over each label's cost on each slot plus half the cheapest possible arrangement of its pair partners around that slot. The assignment is solved exactly in integers and re-solved by OR-Tools' linear sum assignment; both agree.
* **Cell LP** (`search/lp_bound.py`): the first-level RLT relaxation aggregated to the 19 finger-row cells of the 52 slots (B depends only on cells): joint cell distributions for every label pair, consistent with both labels' cells, and with i in cell c the other labels exactly fill every cell d. Solved by PDLP; the bound is **not** the solver's objective but a weak-duality bound recomputed in exact integer arithmetic from the duals (stored in `certificates/<style>-lp-duals.json.gz` and re-checked by `evaluate-phase4`), so it holds whatever the solver's tolerances. GLOP (simplex) did not solve this 481,390-column LP in 30 minutes; PDLP (first-order) did, and its slightly inexact duals cost only the difference between 'solver objective' and 'certified' below.
* **CP-SAT, full problem**: the solver's best bound after the time limit, from the Phase 3 layout as a hint. It is a solver claim: re-running CP-SAT would be replication, not independent proof (RESEARCH_SPEC 8.5). Its model is checked against exhaustive enumeration in section 4.

## 7. Repeated presses in B and D (owner rule, 10 October 2026)

Phase 3 left an owner point: B and D could not type a vowel sign followed by the same vowel's independent form (তিনিই). The owner's timing-free rule: consecutive presses of one vowel key whose first press gave a sign are split into pairs, with one single press first when their number is odd; a single is the sign, a pair the independent vowel (3 presses → ি ই, 4 → ই ই, 5 → ি ই ই). The next different key ends the run. It is implemented in the typing machine (`bangla/styles.py`) and, separately, in the independent verifier. Phase 3 and the cross-check were regenerated with it: no label of any best layout moved; B and D J1 rose by about 0.0003 because these words are now typed instead of escaped. C is unchanged (its three-press rule is not defined).

## 8. What is and is not established

* Every style's Phase 3 layout is a **bounded near-optimum** of its stated scope: no layout of that scope is better by more than the gap in section 1, and the bound is certified by exact arithmetic, independently of solver tolerances.
* Within the listed neighbourhoods the layouts are **proven optimal**. Outside them, a better layout may exist; nothing here rules it out beyond the gap.
* The certificates hold for the training frequencies and the model parameters of Phase 1. Section 5 shows how far the conclusions survive other parameters; it proves nothing.
* Not covered: the third (conjunct) layer, other keyboards, and typing tests with people (Phase 5).
